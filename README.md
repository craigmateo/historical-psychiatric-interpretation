## Overview

This repository contains the research materials, datasets, timelines, bibliographic resources, and reproducible analyses supporting the accompanying paper.

Rather than serving as a draft of the manuscript itself, the repository documents the evidence and research process underlying the historical and philosophical arguments presented in the paper.

The project explores how psychiatric explanations of Friedrich Hölderlin's life have been constructed, transmitted, and interpreted over more than two centuries. Using Hölderlin as a historical case study, it investigates broader questions concerning retrospective diagnosis, medical authority, historical interpretation, and the philosophy of psychiatry.

## Research Questions

The project investigates questions including:

- How have psychiatric interpretations of Hölderlin changed over time?
- What evidence has been used to support retrospective diagnoses?
- How have later authors relied upon earlier diagnostic traditions?
- How do psychiatric explanations interact with literary, philosophical, political, and historical interpretations?
- What can the Hölderlin case teach us about psychiatric explanation more generally?

## Guiding Principles

This repository emphasizes:

- Transparency of research methods
- Reproducibility where possible
- Clear separation between primary evidence and interpretation
- Open scholarly documentation

The repository is intended as a research companion rather than a substitute for the published paper.

## Conference Presentation

An earlier version of this research was presented at the **13th International Philosophy of Medicine Roundtable Conference**, hosted by the University of Pittsburgh's Institute for Bioethics.

**Presentation:** *Hölderlin and the Problem of Psychiatric Explanation: A Case Study in Historical Interpretation*  
**Presenter:** Craig Frayne  
**Presentation date:** September 15, 2026  
**Conference dates:** September 15–18, 2026  
**Host institution:** University of Pittsburgh, Institute for Bioethics  
**Format:** Online presentation

[Official conference announcement](https://bioethics.pitt.edu/2026/08/26/13th-international-philosophy-of-medicine-roundtable-conference/)

The conference presentation introduced the project's central question: how psychiatric explanations acquire interpretive authority over human lives. Using the historical reception of Friedrich Hölderlin, it examined how psychiatric diagnoses can become inherited explanatory frameworks that shape subsequent interpretations of biography, literature, and historical evidence.

The accompanying paper remains in development, and the repository documents the continuing research.
